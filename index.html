Introduction

A Regional Milestone in
Ecumenical Formation

NATI-RETI 2026 is a fully immersive residential theological learning program designed as a pilgrimage across borders — bringing together emerging Christian leaders from the United States and Canada for deep theological reflection and spiritual fellowship.

Drawing inspiration from the World Council of Churches’ Global Ecumenical Theological Institutes (GETI) — which have gathered emerging theologians in Busan, Arusha, Karlsruhe, Abuja, and Wadi El Natrun — NATI-RETI adapts this proven model of immersion and dialogue to the specific context of North America-Turtle Island.

This initiative marks a significant renewed chapter in cross-border collaboration, creating a shared platform to engage the deep intersections of faith and public life, and to nurture the visible unity of the church.

Central Theme

“Where Now for
Visible Justice?”

This question serves as the common thread weaving through every plenary, study group, study visit, and intercultural contextual immersion. It challenges North American churches to move beyond abstract affirmations and confront the tangible requirements of the Gospel in their specific contexts.

Whether discussing climate, race, politics, economics, or solidarity with indigenous communities, the question of justice—and the church’s role in embodying it—will intersect with all theological and ecumenical reflections.

Curriculum

Six Thematic Pillars

Guided by the central theme of visible justice, the curriculum addresses six critical dimensions grounded in the lived realities of North American–Turtle Island communities.

01

Climate Justice

Theological and practical responses to the ecological crisis, integrating care for creation with the church’s commitment to justice for all peoples.

02

Secularism and Laicity

Navigating faith and public witness in increasingly secularized societies, and discerning the church’s voice and role in the public square.

03

Racism and Anti-Racism

Addressing systemic injustice and the church’s responsibility in dismantling white supremacy and building communities of genuine equity and belonging.

04

Interfaith Dialogue

Building meaningful bridges with neighbours of living faiths in our pluralistic societies, and discerning the ecumenical calling to peaceful coexistence.

05

Solidarity with Indigenous Communities

Understanding and exploring the legacy of colonialism and the church’s path toward genuine reconciliation with Indigenous peoples of Turtle Island.

06

Wealth Disparity and Poverty

Analyzing economic inequality and inequity within two of the world’s wealthiest nations through the lens of prophetic theology and Gospel witness.

Pedagogical Approach

Three-Tiered Methodology

Adapted from the GETI 2025 Curriculum, all NATI-RETI modules employ a three-tiered methodological approach ensuring learning is simultaneously intellectually rigorous, spiritually grounded, and contextually relevant.

Intellectual

Engaging with theological texts, historical documents, and contextual analyses relevant to North American–Turtle Island realities. Critical examination of dominant narratives and their relationship to the Gospel call for justice.

Spiritual

Grounding intellectual engagement in prayer, lament, worship, and theological reflection. Incorporating diverse spiritual traditions represented in the ecumenical community, including Indigenous, Black, and settler church expressions.

Contextual

Connecting learning to specific communities and contexts in Detroit and Toronto as living classrooms. Exploring the lived realities of Indigenous, racialized, migrant, and marginalized communities through site visits and community encounters.

Certification

Academic Credit & Accreditation

For participants who wish to integrate this pilgrimage into their formal theological education, NATI-RETI 2026 offers the opportunity to earn academic credit at the Toronto School of Theology.

This optional accreditation formally recognizes the rigorous contextual and ecumenical engagement required during the two-week institute.

Read Complete Academic Guidelines

Transferable Credit

Earn formal academic credit equivalent in ecumenical theology, which may be applied toward your ongoing degree programs.

Integrative Assessment

Credit evaluation is seamlessly woven into the methodology, based on active immersion, collaborative presentations, and a final theological paper.

Official Certification

Whether pursuing academic credit or attending for personal leadership development, all participants who complete the full program receive an official NATI-RETI Certificate of Completion.

Program Staff

Facilitation Team & Program Staff · NATI-RETI 2026

The Rev. Dr. Loida I. Martell is Vice President of Academic Affairs and Dean Emerita, and Professor of Constructive Theology Emerita of Lexington Theological Seminary. Dr. Martell served as the 18th VPAA/Dean of Lexington Theological Seminary and joined its faculty in August of 2017, and retired in August of 2025. She is a Doctor in Veterinary Medicine and ordained in the American Baptist Churches/USA. Dr. Martell is co-editor of Teología en Conjunto: A Collaborative Hispanic Protestant Theology (WJK), and co-author of Latina Evangélicas: A Theological Survey from the Margins (Cascade Books). She is a co-convener for the National Council of Churches Table of Theological Dialogue and Matters of Faith and Order. Dr. Martell was the Distinguished Fellow for the Baptist Scholars International Roundtable for 2024. She was awarded the Lifetime Achievement Award by La Comunidad of Hispanic Scholars of Religion (of AAR/SBL) in 2022. The American Baptist Home Missions Societies awarded Dr. Martell the 2015 Richard Hoiland Christian Education Award, its highest recognition for “faithful and effective leadership in Christian education,” for her long-standing work in nurturing culturally and racially diverse classrooms and for exceptional leadership.

Soren M. Hessler is Assistant Professor in the Practice of Leadership and Administration, Director of the Master of Arts in Religion and Leadership Program, and Co-Director of the Atlanta Rabbinic Leadership Lab at Candler School of Theology, Emory University. Hessler earned his Ph.D. in practical theology at Boston University. His research emerges at the intersections of higher education administration, ecumenical and interreligious studies, and leadership theory. He serves as treasurer of the Association for Interreligious/Interfaith Studies and as treasurer for Interreligious Studies Media, the parent organization of the Journal of Interreligious Studies. He is also co-general editor of Methodist Review. Hessler is an elder in full connection in the West Ohio Annual Conference of the United Methodist Church and serves as co-convenor of the Christian Education, Faith Formation, and Leadership Development Convening Table of the National Council of Churches of the Churches in Christ in the USA.

Rubén David is a Ph.D. candidate at Emmanuel College of Victoria University in the University of Toronto. He is a Visiting Professor at Lexington Theological Seminary and the Seminario Evangélico de Puerto Rico, a Seasonal Lecturer at Emmanuel College, and the Chief Editor of the Baptist Peacemaker, the church journal of the Baptist Peace Fellowship of North America. He has also worked as a Research Fellow, collaborating on archival research related to the processes of colonization and conquest in Latin America and the Caribbean.

Rooted in his Puerto Rican identity and history, Rubén David’s scholarship explores the intersections of colonial processes in Latin America and the Caribbean. His research interests include decolonial thinking and theology, Latin American liberation theology, studies on imagination and dreams, identity theories, and gender and sexuality studies, with a special emphasis on masculinities. His doctoral research investigates the colonial influences on contemporary understandings of what it means to “be a man” in Latin America and the Caribbean. Rubén David publishes peer-reviewed articles, book chapters, and book reviews, and serves as a translator for academic articles and conferences in Anglo-North America. He was awarded the Forum for Theological Exploration Doctoral Fellowship in both 2024 and 2025, as well as the 2024 Honorary Dissertation Fellowship from the Louisville Institute. He lives in Toronto with his wife Leslie and their two daughters, Beatriz and Julieta.

Rev. Jennifer Susanne Leath, Ph.D. is the Queen’s National Scholar and Associate Professor in Black Religions at Queen’s University (Kingston, Ontario, Canada); she is also the pastor of Tanner-Price AME Church (Windsor, Ontario, Canada). Prior to Queen’s, Dr. leath served on the faculty of Iliff School of Theology (Denver, CO) as the Assistant Professor of Religion and Social Justice and Director of the Master of Arts in Social Justice and Ethics degree program after completing her A.B. at Harvard University, her M.Div. at Union Theological Seminary in the City of New York, and her Ph.D. at Yale University. Dr. leath also helped establish the Center on African American Religion, Sexual Politics, and Social Justice (CARSS) at Columbia University, serving as its first Director of Research. Dr. leath was a fellow in the Women’s Studies in Religion Program at Harvard Divinity School (2014-2015).

Rev. leath is an Itinerant Elder in the African Methodist Episcopal (AME) Church and, prior to Tanner-Price, she pastored Campbell AME Church (Media, PA), Allen AME Church (White Plains, NY), Campbell Chapel AME Church (Denver, CO). Pastor leath expresses her love for all people through her passion for justice and dismantling all forms of oppression. She especially demonstrates her unequivocal commitments to equity, diversity, inclusion, and indigeneity through her sexual justice work within the AME Church and Black religious traditions more broadly as well as through her research and writing. Beyond her numerous articles, Dr. leath’s first monograph, Black, Quare, and Then to Where: Theories of Justice and Black Sexual Ethics, was published in Fall 2023 (Duke University Press). Through this book, leath introduces and develops “quare” possibilities for flourishing Black futures. Also passionate about the ways diverse Christian traditions can pursue and promote justice together through ecumenical engagement, Dr. leath serves as a Co-Moderator of the Joint Consultative Group between the World Council of Churches (WCC) and Pentecostal Churches and a member of the Central Committee of the WCC.

Deivit is a passionate advocate for intercultural leadership, a path he’s walked personally and professionally. Currently serving as Associate Secretary for Intercultural Leadership at the Council, he brings three years of experience as Teaching, Research, and Program Coordinator from the Forum for Intercultural Leadership and Learning (FILL). Deivit is a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Toronto, where his research explores the profound intersections between economics, theology, decolonial thinking, and ethics. This academic pursuit complements his diverse practical background, which includes roles as an Educational Consultant for the World Council of Churches’ Global Ecumenical Theological Institute (GETI) and as the Latin American Program Director for the NGO Globethics. These experiences have solidified Deivit’s conviction that intercultural understanding is a catalyst for transformative change. He envisions interculturality as a bridge, connecting faith with social action to create a more just and equitable world for all.

Thea grew up in the United Church of Canada, where she has worked as a coordinator and mentor for various youth and social justice programs. She is also a member of the General Council executive, and is the President-Elect for the Pacific Mountain Regional Council. Convening Bodies

CCC

Canadian Council of Churches

The Canadian Council of Churches (CCC) is a broad and inclusive ecumenical body, now representing 26-member churches, including Anglican, Eastern and Roman Catholic, Evangelical, Free Church, Eastern and Oriental Orthodox, and Historic Protestant traditions. Together, these member churches comprise 85% of the Christians in Canada. The Canadian Council of Churches was founded in 1944.
